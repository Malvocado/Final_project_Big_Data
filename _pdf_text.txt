Term Project: Big Data 
1. Overview & Learning Objectives 
This term project asks you to build a complete analytics pipeline for a fictional mid-size online 
retailer, “UrbanCart,” moving from raw, imperfect data all the way to a decision-ready report. It is 
intentionally designed so that SQL, NumPy, and pandas are not optional add-ons but load-bearing 
parts of the workflow; you cannot finish the project well using only one of the three. 
2. Business Scenario: UrbanCart 
UrbanCart is a fictional online retailer selling six product categories (Electronics, Home & Kitchen, 
Apparel, Sports & Outdoors, Beauty & Health, Books & Media) across fifteen cities worldwide. 
UrbanCart’s leadership team has asked you — acting as their newly hired data analyst — to answer 
three big questions: 
1. Who are our most valuable customers, and what drives their loyalty (or churn)? 
2. Which products and categories are actually profitable once returns, discounts, and cost are 
accounted for — and can we forecast near-term demand? 
3. Is our marketing/signup data (spread across an old CRM export and the current database) 
trustworthy enough to make decisions on, and if not, how should it be fixed? 
Your final report is the deliverable leadership will read. Treat the dataset as “real”: it contains the 
kinds of errors, duplicates, and inconsistencies that show up in actual company data, and part of 
your job is noticing and handling them — not assuming the data is clean. 
3. Dataset & Schema 
You will be provided with a SQLite database, ecommerce.db, plus two external CSV files that are 
deliberately NOT already integrated into the database. Reconciling them is part of the assignment. A 
full data dictionary is included in README_DATA.md alongside the files.

4. Project Requirements 
4.1 Phase 1 — SQL Data Extraction 
Write and submit a single .sql file containing the following queries, run directly against 
ecommerce.db. Each query must be preceded by a one-line comment explaining what it answers. 
1. Total revenue, order count, and average order value by product category (net of discounts). 
2. The top 20 customers by lifetime spend, including their city and signup date, using a JOIN 
across customers, orders, and order_items. 
3. Month-over-month revenue trend for the last 24 months using a window function (e.g., LAG or 
a running total). 
4. For each product category, the return rate (share of order_items with negative quantity) — 
written as a subquery or CTE. 
5. Customers who have placed orders in every one of the last 3 calendar quarters (a query 
requiring HAVING with a COUNT of distinct periods). 
6. The 10 products with the highest average review rating that also have at least 15 reviews 
(JOIN + GROUP BY + HAVING). 
7. Average session duration and pages viewed by device type, restricted to customers who made 
at least one purchase (subquery/EXISTS). 
8. A RANK() or DENSE_RANK() query ranking products by revenue within each category. 
9. Payment-method mix (share of orders) split by country. 
10. One query of your own design that answers a question you think leadership should care about 
— justify why in a comment. 
Load the results of at least four of these queries into pandas (via sqlite3 or pandas.read_sql) for use 
in later phases — don’t just paste query outputs; keep them connected to your Python pipeline. 
4.2 Phase 2 — Data Cleaning & Integration with Pandas 
Using pandas, reconcile the three data sources (SQL extracts, legacy_customers_export.csv, 
product_catalog_2024.csv) into clean, analysis-ready DataFrames. Required tasks: 
• Standardize the legacy CSV’s four inconsistent date formats into a single datetime dtype. 
• Detect and resolve duplicate/near-duplicate customer records (exact and fuzzy — e.g., same 
email, different name casing). 
• Decide and justify a policy for missing values in age, city, gender, and review_text (drop vs. 
impute vs. flag) — do not silently fillna(0) everywhere.

• Identify and handle price outliers in products using an IQR or z-score rule; explain your 
threshold choice. 
• Merge product_catalog_2024.csv with the products table on the appropriate keys, reconcile 
column-name/casing differences, and explicitly report which SKUs exist in only one source. 
• Remove exact-duplicate order_items rows and decide how to treat negative quantities (returns) 
in revenue calculations. 
• Reshape data at least once using pivot_table or melt (e.g., a category × month revenue matrix). 
• Produce one time-series analysis using resample or groupby on a datetime index (e.g., weekly

Deliverable for this phase: a documented Jupyter notebook (or .py module) that takes the raw files 
as input and produces clean, saved DataFrames (e.g., as .parquet or .csv) that Phases 3 and 4 read 
from — your pipeline should be re-runnable end to end. 
4.3 Phase 3 — Numerical Analysis with NumPy 
Pandas convenience methods are not allowed for this phase — implement the following directly on 
NumPy arrays so you demonstrate the underlying computation. Show your formulas, not just library 
calls. 
• RFM Segmentation: Bucket customers on Recency, Frequency, and Monetary value; compute 
the three scores as raw NumPy arrays (not via pandas.qcut) and combine them into a single 
segmentation score you define. 
• Similarity / Recommendation: Build a product-similarity or customer-similarity matrix using 
cosine similarity computed with NumPy dot products / norms (no library shortcut such as 
sklearn’s cosine_similarity). Recommend 3 products for 5 sample customers. 
• Regression via Normal Equation: Fit a simple linear model (e.g., predicting monthly revenue 
from month index, or order value from discount level) using the normal equation β = (XᵀX)⁻¹Xᵀy 
implemented with np.linalg, not a fitted library model. Report R² computed manually. 
• Monte Carlo Simulation: Run a Monte Carlo simulation (≥ 5,000 trials) using NumPy’s random 
module to estimate the probability of stocking out for 3 chosen products under demand 
uncertainty; report a confidence interval. 
For each routine, include in your report: the mathematical formula, a short explanation of what it 
measures, and a sanity check against a trivial case or a library function (used only to verify — not to 
compute your final answer). 
4.4 Phase 4 — Business Insights & Visualization 
Using your cleaned data and NumPy routines, answer the following with at least one supporting chart 
each (matplotlib or seaborn): 
1. Which customer segment (from your RFM scores) generates the most revenue, and what 
distinguishes it demographically? 
2. Is there seasonality in overall revenue? Support with a decomposition or rolling-average chart. 
3. Which category has the highest effective margin after accounting for discounts and returns? 
4. Do higher review ratings correlate with repeat-purchase behavior? Quantify the relationship. 
5. Which device type/country combination shows the highest engagement-to-purchase 
conversion? 
6. Based on your regression model, what is the projected revenue for the next 2 months, with an 
uncertainty range?
